# Domain Knowledge Integration

Use this protocol before finalizing hypotheses, methods, interpretation and terminology. Generic statistical competence does not replace subject-matter understanding.

## Contents

- Domain knowledge map
- Source hierarchy
- Terminology and ontology
- Mechanism and plausibility
- Standards and professional constraints
- Interpretation protocol
- Acceptance gate

## Domain Knowledge Map

Build and maintain:

`Knowledge element | Current understanding | Authoritative source | Competing view | Relevance to claim/method | Uncertainty | Manuscript location`

Cover as applicable:

- core entities, constructs, processes and causal or mechanistic relationships;
- established theories and competing explanatory frameworks;
- domain-specific units, scales, notation, thresholds and boundary conditions;
- accepted measurement, sampling, experimental and analytical practices;
- standards, guidelines, taxonomies, ontologies and reporting conventions;
- canonical datasets, benchmarks, reference cases and known failure modes;
- practical workflows, decision contexts and non-use cases;
- ethical, legal, safety, cultural and professional constraints;
- unresolved controversies and evidence gaps.

## Source Hierarchy

Prioritize:

1. current standards, guidelines, official definitions and professional-body documents;
2. high-quality recent reviews, consensus statements and authoritative handbooks;
3. primary studies supporting specific mechanisms, measurements and claims;
4. official datasets, technical reports and validated reference implementations;
5. older foundational works necessary to understand theory or method lineage.

Use informal websites, generic summaries and tertiary explanations only for orientation, never as the sole support for a scientific claim.

## Terminology and Ontology

- Create a terminology ledger with preferred term, definition, synonym, abbreviation, unit and source.
- Use terms consistently across data, code, figures, tables and manuscript.
- Map source-specific labels to a common ontology without erasing meaningful distinctions.
- Distinguish constructs that sound similar but differ operationally.
- Define abbreviations and symbols once and avoid field-inappropriate substitutions.
- Verify translations against authoritative bilingual or domain sources when applicable.

## Mechanism and Plausibility

For each major interpretation, document:

- proposed mechanism or theoretical pathway;
- evidence directly supporting it;
- alternative mechanisms and confounders;
- temporal, physical, biological, behavioral, economic, social, computational or other applicable constraints;
- expected direction, scale and boundary conditions;
- observations that would falsify or weaken the explanation.

Do not infer mechanism from predictive importance, correlation, path significance or visual pattern alone.

## Standards and Professional Constraints

- Identify current applicable standards and reporting guidelines.
- Record edition, issuing body, jurisdiction, scope and access date.
- Check whether data collection, analysis, terminology and figures comply.
- Separate mandatory requirements from common practice and author preference.
- Disclose deviations and explain their effect on validity or applicability.

## Interpretation Protocol

Before writing Discussion:

1. State the result without interpretation.
2. Compare its direction and magnitude with domain expectations and recent evidence.
3. Evaluate plausible mechanisms and alternatives.
4. Check units, scales, thresholds and operational relevance.
5. Identify conditions under which the interpretation fails.
6. Obtain independent specialist review or authoritative cross-check when the claim is high risk or outside the agent's verified knowledge.
7. Narrow the claim when domain knowledge is insufficient.

## Acceptance Gate

Pass only when the domain knowledge map and terminology ledger cover every core claim and method; current standards and authoritative sources have been checked; mechanisms and alternatives are explicit; units and thresholds are correct; known failure modes are addressed; and no interpretation depends solely on generic reasoning.
