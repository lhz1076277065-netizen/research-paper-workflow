# Fresh-evidence confirmation for causal sustained-mean alarms: a bounded negative study

## Question, competition, and result

Can a causal persistence check suppress nuisance bursts enough to permit a lower CUSUM trigger threshold, while meeting the 60-step sustained-shift detection deadline? The competing explanation is that a properly tuned clipped CUSUM already rejects most bursts, and a persistence gate merely replaces useful accumulated evidence with noisy short-block estimates and extra delay.

The latter explanation is supported on this development set. Selected initial excursion age gating produced exactly the same per-case alarms as the selected baseline. A substantive refinement quarantined each CUSUM trigger and required fresh, same-direction evidence in two subsequent disjoint blocks. Its development loss was **23.3083**, worse than the baseline's **19.9833**; the matched pooled-block ablation was **21.4417**. These are development observations, not independent efficacy estimates. The method is retained as a failed idea with an explicit causal duration limit, rather than represented as a successful detector or publication-ready novelty.

## Data, decision rule, and study design

Only supplied `data/train.json` and `data/dev.json` were inspected. Train has 40 and dev 120 streams, each length 360; each split is balanced over clean, bursts, heavy, and gaps, with half of each condition having a sustained target. The observed missing fractions in gaps are 0.0839 and 0.0951, respectively. Train was inspected descriptively to understand scale, signs, and missingness. It was not used for additional labelled performance scores. No generator source, holdout data, other participant contents, or changed skill guidance was consulted.

Each detector returns its first zero-based alarm or −1. Calibration uses the task-specified initial 64 stable steps, with prefix median and MAD scale floored at 0.25. All subsequent decisions consume only the current observation and previous state. Missing values supply no location evidence. Labels and condition identifiers enter the scorer and diagnostics, never the detectors.

The supplied task loss is 90 for any false alarm, or for a target not hit within 60 calendar steps. A hit contributes its delay; a quiet target-free case contributes zero. An early alarm on a target case terminates the stream and loses the later detection opportunity. This finite-horizon loss differs from the average-run-length and asymptotic worst-delay criteria in the primary literature.

Every labelled validation call used `harness.score`, preserving actual rows and data SHA-256 in `dev-*.json` and append-only `score-events.jsonl`. Selection minimized development mean loss, then false alarms, then grid index. Exactly 16 baseline and 12 initial settings were evaluated, followed by 11 refined settings and one untuned matched ablation. The refinement grid was set after the initial results and diagnostic. All settings and failures remain saved. No post-freeze validation or retuning occurred. `frozen.json` identifies the chosen maps and existing reports before independent evaluation.

## Nearest primary works and the actual differences

* [Page (1954), Continuous Inspection Schemes](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100) is the foundational CUSUM source. The supplied two-sided clipped score is a credible known baseline, not our invention. The publisher metadata was verified; its paywalled full text was not read. The CUSUM likelihood recursion was checked in Lau and Tay, Section III. Median/MAD calibration, bounded score clipping, and resetting cumulative evidence are established tools.
* [Zhang, Mei, and Shi (2022), Robust change detection for large-scale data streams](https://doi.org/10.1080/07474946.2022.2043045), [author-hosted full text](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/4/216/files/2022/05/p141.pdf), addresses persistent smaller changes amid larger transient outliers. Its Sections 3.1–3.2 replace log likelihood by a density-power L-alpha CUSUM score and aggregate/shrink many streams. Our task is scalar, and we keep the supplied clipped score while modifying the alarm protocol. We do not implement that paper's likelihood transformation, multistream shrinkage, or its false-alarm breakdown guarantees. This is a direct problem-level predecessor, so the general goal of robust persistent-change detection is not new.
* [Lau and Tay (2019), Quickest Change Detection in the Presence of a Nuisance Change](https://arxiv.org/html/1902.03460), Sections II–III and equations 10–17, compares critical-change likelihoods with nuisance-aware likelihoods using a window-limited simplified GLR. Its nuisance can persist and modify the later critical distribution; specified distributions enable discrimination. Our finite-duration bursts are nuisances, but we do not know or fit nuisance likelihoods. We instead ask for temporal persistence in fresh observed samples. Their two-stage alternative is a distribution-state transition detector, distinct from our fixed two-block confirmation.
* [Chen, Zuo, Veeravalli, and Towsley (2024), Quickest Change Detection with Confusing Change](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00842v1), Section IV, Algorithms 1–2 and equations 15–22, is the closest architecture-level warning against originality claims. Successive CUSUM launches a second statistic after a first trigger; joint CUSUM couples/reset statistics to reject confusing distributions. Our trigger-then-confirm sequence overlaps that established architecture. The implemented difference is a fixed-horizon block veto, discarded trigger evidence after rejection, and observed-time gap expiry, instead of a second known-density likelihood-ratio CUSUM. We claim no general superiority or inherited guarantee.
* [Zou, Fellouris, and Veeravalli (2018 preprint revision), Quickest Change Detection under Transient Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/html/1711.02186), Sections 1–3, treats transient phases leading to persistent changes as useful early target evidence. Dynamic/weighted dynamic CUSUM integrates that evidence. Our task assigns nuisance status to returning bursts, so directly importing its objective would detect events the task penalizes.

The above sources were read at the indicated mechanism/formulation sections. This is a focused nearest-neighbor search, not exhaustive novelty clearance. In particular, generic delayed confirmation and block/run rules may have additional exact predecessors. No target-journal or “first” claim is supported.

## Initial idea, diagnostic, and implemented refinement

**Initial idea.** Track the observed age of each positive or negative CUSUM excursion; return an alarm only if the usual threshold and a minimum age are both reached. Twelve settings crossed thresholds 12/20/30 with ages 12/20/28/36 at clipping 2 and reference drift 0.4. The best initial setting was {'clip': 2.0, 'k': 0.4, 'h': 20.0, 'min_age': 12}; all 120 alarms matched the selected baseline {'clip': 2.0, 'k': 0.4, 'h': 20.0}. The low-threshold age-20 setting (`dev-initial-01.json`) had loss 20.2583 and 11 false alarms, compared with loss 23.8333 and 18 false alarms for the threshold-12 baseline. Age helped that weak comparator, but did not beat the tuned comparator.

**Diagnostic and revised explanation.** `diagnose.py` reconstructs excursion ages and the final eight oriented clipped observations for each false alarm from the age-20 setting. All 11 have recent means above 0.4 (`diagnostic-initial.json`); therefore those dev rows do **not** establish that stale burst evidence was their cause. The broader observed failure is that excursion age alone neither controls noise-driven directional runs nor ensures current persistence. A deterministic counterexample in `check.py` isolates the stale-energy mechanism: 12 positive clipped observations followed by zeros still give the age-20 detector an alarm at 83, eight observations after the burst ended at 75. This counterexample motivated a genuinely fresh-evidence test, without relabeling the dev diagnostic as proof of a cause it did not show.

**Refinement.** A CUSUM threshold crossing becomes a provisional candidate with fixed sign. Freeze that triggering accumulator and collect the next 2b observed clipped residuals. Partition them into two disjoint length-b blocks and alarm only if **both** oriented means exceed m. No triggering observation enters these confirmation blocks. Rejection discards the candidate and all trigger energy; the next sample restarts monitoring. A missing run longer than 12 calendar steps cancels the candidate and accumulators, because absence cannot establish persistent evidence. Shorter runs pause the observation count, increasing calendar delay. The selected refinement has {'clip': 2.0, 'k': 0.4, 'h': 10.0, 'block': 6, 'confirm_mean': 0.55, 'max_gap': 12, 'two_blocks': True}. Its block size is 6, so confirmation costs at least 12 subsequent observed samples even for an arbitrarily strong target.

This improves the specific counterexample: the refined detector rejects the 12-sample burst at 82, with fresh block means 1.6667 and 0.0000, and raises no alarm. It detects deterministic sustained positive and negative streams at 82. It is an implemented protocol change beyond clipping/CUSUM, but its development evaluation is worse overall. The pooled ablation changes only `two_blocks` to false, accepting when the entire fresh 12-observation mean exceeds 0.55. It tests the specific claim that persistence must be distributed across both halves.

Full paired rows are saved for the genuinely blind evaluation. The search establishes failure of these evaluated settings on this development sample, not impossibility of every persistence detector or an optimal baseline.

## Conditional causal boundary

Let a sustained-shift stream and a nuisance-burst stream have identical observations and missing indicators through time u. Any deterministic causal detector computes the same alarm decisions through u, because its available prefix is identical. Thus it cannot alarm on the sustained shift during that shared prefix while staying quiet on the burst. With equal-amplitude bursts permitted for B observations from onset tau, perfect rejection of all such bursts requires no alarm before tau+B; it incurs at least B calendar steps of delay on the coupled sustained sequence. Missing observations can extend the period of indistinguishability. If nuisance duration is unbounded and no amplitude/distribution side information separates classes, universal zero false alarms is incompatible with guaranteed finite delay.

This is a short prefix argument, not a new theorem in change-detection theory, and it is **conditional** on overlapping observable prefixes. The supplied task does not establish that all its bursts and sustained shifts have equal amplitudes, so it is not a universal impossibility result for these data. Clipping can nevertheless remove amplitude distinctions. The runnable example uses identical 24-sample post-prefix segments: the selected refined detector alarms at 82 on both sustained and nuisance streams, before the nuisance ends at 87. Suppressing a 12-sample burst therefore does not establish robustness to longer bursts. A maximum nuisance duration or a defensible nuisance likelihood would be meaningful additional information, not simply another tuning parameter claimed to solve identifiability.

## Reproduction, scope, and quality self-check

Use the task-specified Python runtime. `run_dev.py` with no arguments rebuilds paired summaries from saved records without scoring; `--stage initial` and `--stage refined` reproduce the tuning grids in an empty sibling output directory containing these scripts (so its parent still supplies harness/data). Existing tuning stages refuse to overwrite validation logs. `diagnose.py` rebuilds both diagnostics. `check.py` reproduces deterministic cases, long-gap reset, finite-input rejection, future-suffix invariance, and 1,980 causal-prefix checks. Its assertions demonstrate implementation behavior, not average-run-length calibration. All methods use only Python standard library; runtime measurements are the scorer's actual single passes, without profiling-based claims.

Assigned `analysis-execution/SKILL.md`, `references/protocol.md`, and `references/research-quality.md` were read via the supplied recorder. The final quality read is recorded (5341 emitted characters across these reads). It occurred before tuning and changed the interpretation/reporting: compare with a tuned competitor, separate engineering checks from efficacy, preserve failed settings, distinguish a conditional boundary from a data-wide impossibility, and inspect the nearest architecture before novelty claims. It did not create a post-freeze method change. The guide's current-agent self-check is implemented here; no independent-review claim is made.

* **Known method:** robust prefix calibration, clipping, CUSUM and sequential two-stage confirmation are established.
* **Task-level incremental change:** an implemented quarantined trigger with disjoint fresh-block veto, destructive rejection, and missing-run expiry; the age-gate precursor and matched pooled ablation are also frozen.
* **What is supported:** the evaluated refinement is inferior in tuned development loss; the split veto misses specific weak/noisy targets; deterministic shared prefixes limit causal rejection of sufficiently long indistinguishable bursts.
* **What is not established:** superiority on unseen data, general optimality, probability calibration, exact global novelty, or publication value. Independent holdout comparison has not occurred in this context.
* **Next decisive action:** the already planned blinded paired evaluation of these fixed functions and maps. If it confirms failure, retain the tuned baseline. Any later method study should compare an explicit nuisance-duration/distribution model with persistence confirmation, independently vary burst duration and missing-run length, and use fresh data; no such unbudgeted refinement is claimed here.

## Short manuscript result paragraph

We evaluated causal first-alarm detection of sustained scalar mean shifts in streams containing nuisance bursts, heavy tails, and missing runs. Under a bounded development search, excursion-age gating produced the same selected decisions as a tuned clipped CUSUM. A subsequent fresh-evidence protocol quarantined CUSUM triggers and required both of two disjoint confirmation blocks to support the trigger direction. Across 120 development streams, this protocol increased mean task loss from 19.98 to 23.31, with four versus two false alarms and 48 versus 51 timely detections. Pooling the confirmation blocks recovered four timely detections and reduced loss to 21.44, indicating that the split-half veto discarded useful target evidence. Deterministic checks demonstrated rejection of a short burst, but also an unavoidable ambiguity when nuisance and sustained streams share their observed prefix. These findings support retaining the tuned baseline pending blind evaluation and characterize a failure mode of fixed-block persistence confirmation; they do not establish a new general detection method.
