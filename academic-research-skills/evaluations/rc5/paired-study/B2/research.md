# Burst erasure for causal first alarms: a development study with a negative baseline comparison

**Question and scientific commitment.** Can temporal structure of nuisance bursts help a causal detector distinguish sustained scalar shifts more effectively than a well-tuned, bounded-influence CUSUM when heavy tails and missing runs occur? The proposed increment is to demand evidence that survives deleting the strongest contiguous short block. The decisive comparison is first-alarm loss on the same cases, including nuisance-only streams; speed on detected shifts alone cannot establish success. A competing explanation is that block deletion merely imposes an expensive effective threshold and finite-memory constraint, losing weak shifts while suppressing some false alarms. The evidence here favors retaining the known baseline. This is a completed development study, not an independently validated superiority claim or publication-ready new-method claim.

**Data and target.** The supplied train set has 40 streams and dev has 120; each has length 360 and four conditions, clean, bursts, gaps, and heavy, with half the streams in each condition having sustained targets. Each stream has the stipulated stable 64-step prefix, with no missing calibration entries in these files. Dev contains 30 cases and 15 targets per condition. Overall missing fractions in gaps are 0.08389 on train and 0.09509 on dev. Metadata summaries are saved separately. The detector receives only scalar values/None and parameters; condition, case ID, target existence, and tau never enter its decision. No generator source or holdout was read. Dev labels were used for adaptive selection; therefore all reported performance is development evidence.

The supplied scorer charges loss 90 for an alarm before tau, any alarm on a no-target stream, or a target not detected within 60 steps; an in-window true alarm costs its delay, and a correctly silent no-target stream costs zero. This combines delay and errors on different units into the task's stipulated decision loss. A 60-step hit is an operational label, not consistency or mean-shift identification. In particular, an alarm more than 60 steps after tau is a miss under this loss even if it responds to the shift.

**Nearest primary work and mechanism distinctions.** [Page (1954), Continuous Inspection Schemes](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/41.1-2.100), is the source of CUSUM. The supplied median/MAD-calibrated clipped two-sided recurrence is the known baseline, not a contribution. Its increments, reference drift, reflection at zero, and threshold are ordinary CUSUM ingredients. The publisher record was checked; the full historical paper was not accessible.

[Lucas and Crosier (1982), Robust CUSUM](https://doi.org/10.1080/03610928208828414), already evaluated modified CUSUM schemes under contaminated normals and described suppressing a first suspected outlier while signaling on successive outliers. The publisher abstract was available through search, but direct full-text access failed. It warns against presenting persistence confirmation or clipping as new. My initial method instead waits after a candidate, tests a later block, and resets after failure; neither confirmation in general nor this particular composition has established novelty.

The closest target-level predecessor is [Zhang, Mei and Shi (2022), Robust change detection for large-scale data streams](https://doi.org/10.1080/07474946.2022.2043045), with [author-hosted full text](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.gatech.edu/dist/4/216/files/2022/05/p141.pdf). Their goal includes persistent smaller changes amid transient larger outliers. Their local La-CUSUM uses a Box-Cox transformation of density evidence and combines streams by shrinkage; their contamination model and false-alarm breakdown criterion provide theory. Here there is one scalar stream, a finite calibration prefix, a time-contiguous erasure operation, and a finite-horizon task loss. I do not inherit their robustness theorem or establish comparable breakdown properties. Abstract, model section 2, and local statistic section 3.1 were read.

[Sokolov, Spivak and Tartakovsky (2023 arXiv version), Detecting an Intermittent Change of Unknown Duration](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.17342), derives CUSUM, window-limited CUSUM, and finite moving average rules from likelihood formulations. Its finite moving sum, section 3.3 equation 23, is a direct ancestor of the erasure-free comparator. Its transient changes are detection targets; ours are nuisance. Subtracting the strongest block reverses the role of concentrated transient evidence. The paper also emphasizes local false-alarm probability, which this study does not estimate or control.

[Bhatt, Fang and Li (2022), Offline Change Detection under Contamination](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v180/bhatt22a.html), uses robust influence-function mean estimates in a scan under correlated contamination. Its abstract and first-page mechanism description were read. That is an offline multiple-change problem, not a first stopping time; nevertheless robust scanning under contamination is established prior art. [Xie, Moustakides and Xie (2023 arXiv version), Window-Limited CUSUM](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.06777), combines CUSUM with sliding estimation of unknown post-change parameters. Its abstract and problem discussion were checked. My refinement does not estimate a post-change model or claim its asymptotic optimality. This is a focused nearest-neighbor search, not an exhaustive novelty clearance.

**Initial implementation and diagnostic.** Both proposed detectors fix center to the prefix median and scale to max(0.25, 1.4826 × prefix MAD), shared with the baseline. Observed standardized values are clipped to ±2; missing values add no evidence. The initial method has a separate state per direction. A CUSUM crossing h=10 starts a candidate; after a four-step guard, the next twelve wall-clock steps must contain at least seven observations and signed clipped mean above 0.55. A failed test resets that direction's score. The complete 12-configuration grid varied guards 4/8/12, confirmation length 12/18, and mean threshold 0.35/0.55/0.75 in the combinations saved in run_dev.py.

This hypothesis failed. The selected initial detector had loss 24.000 versus baseline 19.350, six false alarms versus four, and 48 timely hits versus 52. On paired cases it improved nine, worsened 41, and tied 70. initial-diagnostic.json records condition comparisons; initial-false-traces.json saves actual standardized trailing observations and calibration estimates for its six false alarms. One new nuisance-only burst-condition error was case 65109-bursts-22, alarm 182: its trailing trace contains extended negative evidence across both guard and confirmation, rather than an isolated single enormous value. A fixed delayed mean does not reject every temporally concentrated nuisance/noise excursion. Heavy case 65109-heavy-6 also added a new false alarm and had a prefix scale estimate 0.529. These traces show the response shape; without inspecting the generator they do not prove which individual points were intentionally generated burst values. The initial candidate threshold is also lower than the selected baseline's threshold, so not all degradation can be attributed to confirmation alone.

**Refinement and exact implemented quantity.** Let e_i^d = d clip((x_i−mu)/scale, ±c)−k for observed values, and zero for missing values, for d in {−1,+1}. For each current trailing wall-clock window I_t, compute

R_t^d = sum_{i in I_t} e_i^d − max(0, max over contiguous B-step blocks J contained in I_t of sum_{i in J} e_i^d).

Before a full B-step window exists the block width is min(B, |I_t|). The selected block is the first maximizing block in a tie; its actual observed count is removed from the support count. The first current observed time with R_t^d > h and at least min_count retained observations alarms. erase=0 removes the inner maximization. This is a clipped finite moving sum with adversarial block subtraction and an observed-support gate, not another name for clipping or a CUSUM recurrence. It recomputes a length-W window with a rolling B-step sum, O(W) time and memory per observation; no specialized dependency is needed. Missing runs expire evidence in wall-clock time, unlike baseline evidence retained across gaps.

The 12 refined configurations include 10 erasure configurations and two erasure-free ablations, all scored and recorded. The selected refinement has W=32, B=8, k=0.2, h=10, min_count=16, clip=2. Baseline uses k=0.35, h=20, clip=2, chosen from 16 combinations of four k values and four h values. Selection minimized dev loss, breaking ties by false alarms then hits; erasure-free configurations were excluded from selecting the proposed refinement. Grids and selected record names are explicit in run_dev.py and frozen.json. The selection rule for refinement was fixed before its scores. The ablation is matched to W=48, h=10 erasure, rather than to the selected W=32 method, because those paired configurations were included within the original 12-config budget.

**Causal checks and established limits.** run_dev.py --check checks every prefix length 0 through 360 on all 40 training streams for initial and refined methods: 28,880 exact prefix-consistency assertions passed. These use no scorer and no selection. Any detector alarm on a full stream must appear at the same index on every sufficiently long prefix and remain absent on shorter prefixes. Known cases reject isolated bursts of duration 1, 4, and 8 in both directions, detect sustained ±2 shifts at index 102 after a true onset at 84, reject all-missing tails, return −1 on an empty input, and reject NaN. A long-gap construction alarms at observed index 157; missing values never become positive evidence. checks.json records actual output and check runtime. These assertions do not certify all possible sequences or statistical error rates; root's unseen evaluation remains decisive.

A limited erasure fact is established directly. If within a window all positive directional increments lie in one contiguous block of length at most B, and every increment outside it is nonpositive, choose a B-step block covering that positive support. The maximum removed sum is at least its sum. Thus the surviving sum is at most the sum outside that covering block, which is nonpositive; a positive h cannot alarm. A partially filled window shorter than B has no retained support. This does not protect against positive baseline noise, multiple separated bursts, an inaccurate prefix center, or burst duration exceeding B. It is an algebraic property of the implementation's statistic, not a probability bound.

There is also a basic causal indistinguishability limit: a sustained shift and a transient burst with exactly the same values through its end have identical observed prefixes. Any causal alarm during that common prefix occurs on both. Therefore rejecting every burst through length D forbids alarm in the first D observations of its indistinguishable sustained counterpart. The runnable construction finds sustained strong-shift alarm 99 and constructs a 16-step burst ending at 99 that alarms identically. This is a demonstrated ceiling, not an implementation bug. Future work must specify a nuisance-duration class or accept a probabilistic rather than universal burst-rejection claim.

**Manuscript result paragraph.** On 120 supplied development streams, bounded-influence CUSUM achieved mean first-alarm loss 19.350 with 52 of 60 target shifts detected within 60 steps and four false alarms. A candidate/holdoff/confirmation design increased loss to 24.000. Replacing that design with finite-window evidence after deleting its strongest eight-step block reduced loss to 20.400, but did not surpass CUSUM and produced seven false alarms. Matched erasure ablations demonstrated substantial false-alarm suppression at increased delay. Prefix checks and a constructed shared-prefix counterexample verified causal implementation and exposed the unavoidable duration-identification tradeoff. These results support block erasure as an interpretable development mechanism, while favoring the known baseline for the supplied loss pending independent evaluation.

**Research-quality self-check and claim boundaries.** The required references/research-quality.md was read through the recorder after tuning and before finalization. It changed interpretation and checks, not the frozen method or selected parameters: I made the baseline failure explicit, distinguished target-level novelty from ordinary ingredients, added the formal shared-prefix limitation, and treated matched ablation evidence as insufficient to establish general benefit. The decisive scientific gap is independent evaluation across burst durations, multiple bursts, prefix bias, and missingness patterns, with a fixed local false-alarm target and matched tuning resources. That requires new data after freeze, rather than more dev search.

Known methods: CUSUM, median/MAD normalization, clipping, finite moving sums, and persistence confirmation. Task-level implemented increment: remove an unknown contiguous nuisance block from signed finite-window evidence, with retained-observation support and wall-clock expiration. Unestablished publication novelty: whether this erasure construction is absent from prior scan/contamination/duration-robust detection work; this search cannot certify that. No journal target, independent-review claim, real-world measurement claim, or optimality claim is warranted. The assigned skill helped preserve the failed first design and require actual paired evidence; it did not supply this problem's algorithm as an example.
