# Research judgement: reliability of apparent estimation gains

This is a design judgement using the fabricated profiles R1–R4 in the supplied research brief. They establish facts **within this synthetic case only**; they are not real publications or evidence of scientific priority. No observations were collected, simulator experiments run, or empirical findings produced.

The scientific objective remains: **determine when an apparent improvement in estimation is reliable, why it can fail, and what evidence can support a decision to use it.** A larger estimator and a new benchmark are possible tools, not the research question.

## What the supplied profiles do and do not establish

| Profile | Supported in this case | Limit on that knowledge |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | An established correction reduces average error when reference measurements are acquired independently of the target outcome. | No evidence for references sharing the target selection process; no stated general precision boundary. Repeating the independent-reference result alone adds little. |
| R2 | A larger estimator has lower reported average error on a random split of the same records, with a post-target proxy included. | The proxy may be unavailable at the intended estimation time. Its contribution and performance under changed acquisition were not tested. The report cannot distinguish better estimation from an information advantage or validation mismatch. |
| R3 | Simple calibration is stable under an independent acquisition change within a narrow precision range. | Stability outside that range is unknown. “Acquisition changed” does not imply stability under outcome-dependent selection. |
| R4 | A careful replication finds the advertised average gain disappears when reference acquisition depends on target selection. | This is evidence for a mechanism-specific failure, not proof that every dependent process defeats every method. It does not provide a truth-free dependence diagnostic or reliability certificate. |

R1 and R4 therefore already make “selection can matter” a weak standalone novelty claim. R3 leaves a precision boundary open. R2 leaves the timing and information explanation open. None licenses “a novel generally robust method.” The closest comparisons here are these profiles; real-literature novelty has not been investigated and must not be asserted.

## A sharper research commitment

Ask: **Under which combinations of reference acquisition and measurement precision does a gain survive a fair evaluation, and can its reliability be assessed from information available before target truth is observed?**

For a future study, define a target population and intended estimation time first. Use only inputs available at that time. Fix a baseline, candidate procedure, and loss before confirmation. For example, with squared error as an explicitly proposed loss, define

\[
\Delta(a,p)=\mathbb{E}_{a,p}\{L(f_0,T)-L(f,T)\},
\]

where \(T\) is target truth, \(a\) specifies the acquisition mechanism, and \(p\) specifies measurement precision. Positive \(\Delta\) means lower expected loss than the baseline. This definition is a proposed evaluation target; the profiles do not specify their exact loss, effect sizes, equations, or precision thresholds. Those details cannot be reconstructed from the summaries.

“Reliable” should mean a gain exceeding a predeclared meaningful margin, with uncertainty accounted for, throughout the **declared** deployment conditions. Report each relevant acquisition/precision regime, including failures, rather than infer robustness from a pooled mean. A diagnostic of dependence and a certificate of positive \(\Delta\) are different tasks: knowing that dependence exists need not determine its effect on error.

Three plausible explanations deserve discrimination. Gains may depend on reference selection (R1/R4), on extra post-target information (R2), or on a favorable precision regime (R3). These explanations can coexist; the design must vary their causes separately instead of force them into mutually exclusive stories.

## Comparison of research routes

| Route | Potential knowledge increment and nearest comparison | Decisive evidence | Why pursue or defer it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Explain the reliability boundary** | Extend R1/R4 from one selection contrast, and R3 from one precision range, to conditions under which gain changes sign. Explain whether precision modifies the effect of selection. | A derivation or valid counterexample for a specified measurement/selection model, followed by controlled simulator contrasts that vary selection while holding precision fixed, and precision while holding selection fixed. | Strong, feasible mechanism work. A result merely reproducing R4 is insufficient; a boundary, interaction, or necessary assumption would add knowledge. A null interaction still narrows the claim if tested over a justified domain. |
| **B. Establish whether reliability can be diagnosed without target truth** | Address R4's unresolved practical obstacle: can available measurements and acquisition records distinguish safe from unsafe conditions, and what extra information is necessary? | Either two admissible processes with the same observable distribution but opposite gain signs, or a proof/bound that gain is identifiable under explicit assumptions. Any proposed diagnostic then needs independent validation, including ambiguous cases. | Highest immediate information value: it determines whether a truth-free diagnostic is possible before effort is spent building one. A valid limitation result can justify an audit or acquisition redesign. It must be scoped to the process family; failure to find a counterexample is not proof. |
| **C. Build a method that improves under declared deployment conditions** | Remove the unresolved information advantage in R2 and demonstrate an ability beyond R1 correction and R3 calibration, such as improved performance in a specified dependent-acquisition regime. | Retrain without the post-target proxy; align permissible inputs, tuning access, and evaluation conditions for all methods; freeze selection before independent acquisition/precision evaluations. Demonstrate the claimed gain and report harmful regimes. | Potentially useful, but premature as the first move. There is no present evidence that model capacity is the limiting factor or that the candidate failure is correctable from available inputs. Defer until A or B identifies a concrete gap a method can repair. |

**Priority: start with B's identifiability challenge, then use A to define or test the resulting boundary.** C remains a conditional route. B is selected for the decision it can change, not because it is the easiest miniature exercise: if available observables cannot support reliability assessment, a larger estimator or more tuning cannot repair that evidential obstacle. Conversely, an identifiable restricted setting makes a focused diagnostic or method study worthwhile. A remains valuable if truth-free certification is impossible; the full objective can still be answered by establishing reliable acquisition designs and their limits.

## The next discriminating work

The next action is a **symbolic identifiability challenge**, with an assumption ledger and a proof or counterexample target. This work is proposed, not performed here.

1. **Specify what is observed and what is constrained.** Define latent truth, target measurements, reference measurements, selection indicators, acquisition records, and precision information. State which records are available before truth and which require an audit. Specify a concrete measurement equation and a fixed correction/calibration exemplar: the summaries do not supply R1/R3 implementations. Distinguish known randomization of acquisition from a claim of independence inferred from data. State whether noise bias and precision are known, estimated independently, or unrestricted.
2. **Try to construct observationally indistinguishable worlds.** Within that stated model family, seek two processes that have the same joint law for all available observables but opposite signs of \(\Delta\) for the same frozen candidate and baseline. Both must respect the measurement and selection assumptions. Arbitrarily replacing hidden truth without respecting those constraints would be an irrelevant toy result. If such a pair exists, any rule using only those observables must have the same output distribution in the two worlds and cannot give a correct decisive gain-sign classification in both. This is a conditional proof target, not an already established impossibility theorem for the case.
3. **Make the outcome change the plan.** A valid pair rejects universal certification within that family: identify the smallest additional assumption or information that separates the pair, such as independently justified measurement calibration, documented acquisition randomization, or a target-truth audit. Test whether that addition actually identifies or bounds the gain; do not merely assume it does. If a lower bound exceeds the meaningful margin, certification is possible within those assumptions. If bounds straddle zero, abstention or additional measurement is an appropriate conclusion. If no pair is found, seek an actual identification proof; search failure alone leaves the question unresolved.
4. **Prepare a conditional simulator pilot.** After this symbolic gate, use the inexpensive manufactured simulator to examine the relevant cases, not to manufacture a general conclusion. A compact starting design crosses independent versus outcome-selective reference acquisition with precision inside versus outside a declared narrow range. Choose and document the range for the simulator rather than claim it is R3's unknown threshold. Include an admissible-input larger estimator and a separate post-target-proxy arm solely to diagnose the R2 explanation. Keep evaluation latent truth sealed from fitting, model selection, and diagnostic construction. Declare any development labels separately and allow them equally when the assumed acquisition setting permits. Use held-out acquisition conditions and fresh realizations, rather than only random record splits.

The pilot should check that the specified mechanisms can produce the intended qualitative anchors and that the contrasts distinguish explanations. Selection sensitivity at fixed precision supports the mechanism route; loss of advantage after proxy removal weakens the capacity explanation; a precision-dependent reversal motivates a restricted boundary. An unexplained discrepancy first requires checking implementation and model assumptions. None of these outcomes would establish field robustness: manufactured processes show what can happen under stated assumptions, not what occurs in an unobserved real system.

For later confirmation, separate exploration and tuning from a locked evaluation. Retain all prespecified contrasts and important negative results; report regime-specific gain, uncertainty, and calibration where relevant. Do not run many configurations and use their best held-out result as confirmation. Exact parameter values, repeat counts, and precision thresholds require the concrete simulator and desired decision precision; inventing them now would add false specificity.

## Replacement plan and claim boundaries

Replace the draft with: “Study the reliability of apparent estimation gains under reference selection and measurement precision. First determine what observable information can identify or bound the gain of a specified baseline comparison. Then use controlled manufactured processes to discriminate selection effects, post-target information advantages, and precision boundaries. Evaluate correction, calibration, and a capacity-based candidate with aligned admissible inputs and independent confirmation. Pursue a new method only if a defined failure remains correctable. Report the conditions of success, failure, and unresolved diagnosis.”

The present deliverable is a justified research direction and a next-work decision. Conditional identifiability, an extended mechanism boundary, and a useful diagnostic are **candidate contributions**. R1–R4 support only their stated findings within the fixed case. No new method has been validated, no pilot has been run, and no novelty, general robustness, or real-world finding is established.
