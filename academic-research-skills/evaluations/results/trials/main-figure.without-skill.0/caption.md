Figure 1. Method ranking varies across strata and acquisition conditions in fixed synthetic results. (a) Row-specific loss estimates for A (blue circles) and B (orange squares), with the supplied 95% intervals reproduced without re-estimation; lower loss is better. Each stratum label gives the supplied n for each method and its within-condition weight w = n / Σn. (b) Stratum composition changes from 80% S1 and 20% S2 under reference acquisition to 20% S1 and 80% S2 under changed acquisition; each condition has total n = 100 per method. (c) Descriptive weighted mean losses Σw × loss: reference, A = 4.80 and B = 4.36; changed, A = 6.60 and B = 7.52. Aggregate intervals were not supplied and are not drawn. B has lower point-estimate loss in S1 under both conditions, whereas A has lower loss in S2 under both conditions. The aggregate ranking reverses, so these results do not support the unconditional claim that “B is a better estimator overall.” Both stratum composition and stratum-specific losses differ between acquisition conditions; the reversal cannot be attributed solely to the change in weights. These fixed results provide no significance test, causal identification, or evidence of a mechanism.
