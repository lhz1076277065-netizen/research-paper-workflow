# When can an apparent estimation gain be trusted?

Prioritize the acquisition-reliability route: determine the conditions under which an apparent gain is informative about deployment error, and when that conclusion must be withheld. Start with an observability and identifiability analysis; use a small controlled simulator pilot only after its conditions and competing predictions are explicit. Keep precision limits and leakage-clean capacity gains as live alternatives.

This is a research judgement based on four **fabricated study profiles**. R1–R4 establish facts only inside this fixed scenario. They are not real publications or evidence of publication priority. No observations were collected, models fitted, or simulator experiments run. The worked example below is an exact constructed thought experiment, not an empirical result.

## Repair the scientific question

The objective remains **the reliability of apparent improvements in a generic estimation system**, rather than winning a new benchmark. A sharper question is:

> Under which acquisition, input-timing, and measurement-precision conditions does a measured improvement over a fixed baseline remain an improvement on an independently evaluated target population? What information can justify that judgement before deployment target truth is available?

For a fixed loss, define the true gain in acquisition condition `a` and precision condition `q` as

`G(a,q) = E[loss(baseline,Y) − loss(candidate,Y) | target population, a, q]`.

Positive gain means lower candidate error. Keep the observed gain from selected records or reference measurements separate from this quantity. A random record split can estimate performance for its sampled conditions; it does not by itself establish the gain in a changed target population. Define the loss and relevant population before comparing methods. Squared error is a transparent choice for the proposed manufactured example, not an unstated property of R1–R4.

Reliability has two obligations: the gain survives the specified changes, and the procedure used to endorse that gain does not systematically certify harmful candidates. If a future diagnostic is proposed, assess the probability that it endorses a gain when true gain is nonpositive, alongside how often it abstains. Detection of dependence alone is insufficient: dependence need not always destroy a gain, and independence does not remove every source of error.

There is no basis here for a universal robustness claim, absolute novelty claim, or probability of publication success.

Route C has the greatest immediate decision value because a larger model cannot supply missing identifying information, and a precision map cannot alone justify conclusions about target selection. This is not a reason to abandon A or B: a clean capacity advantage, a precision interaction, or a lack of observable acquisition information could change the ranking. The contribution sought is a reliability condition with a specified scope, not a newly named combination of existing procedures.

## A concrete caution about the chosen route

Suppose the available observation is `O=(X,R,M)`, where `X` takes values −1 and +1 equally often, the reference `R=X`, and the logged metadata `M` has the same distribution in both worlds. The baseline predicts 0 and the candidate predicts `R`.

* In world A, the hidden truth is `Y=X`: squared-error baseline risk is 1, candidate risk is 0, and gain is +1.
* In world B, the hidden truth is `Y=−X`: baseline risk is 1, candidate risk is 4, and gain is −3.

Both worlds have exactly the same observable law, yet opposite gain signs. A rule using only `O` must have the same output distribution in both; it cannot truthfully certify positive gain in one and reject it in the other with certainty. The two-state arithmetic was checked exactly, without sampling.

This only proves a limitation **for this deliberately unconstrained two-world construction**. It does not prove that every practically motivated diagnostic is impossible, establish the mechanism in R4, or exclude guarantees under additional measurement assumptions. Its role is to expose the missing obligation: specify what connects references or logged acquisition information to truth. Randomized acquisition by itself may help identify selection effects but does not automatically establish measurement validity or guarantee positive gain. Candidate assumptions or additional resources must be justified, not silently supplied by the simulator.

## The next discriminating work

The next work unit is an **observability contract and matched-mechanism design**, before model search. It should leave a short symbolic analysis and an executable pilot specification, not a best-performing model.

1. **Freeze what is observed.** Define target truth `Y`, pre-target covariates `X`, reference measurements `R`, target-selection indicator `S`, reference-acquisition indicator `A`, precision `q`, and input timestamps. List which quantities are visible to the estimator, which to a proposed diagnostic, and which only to an independent evaluator. R2's post-target proxy is excluded from deployment inputs unless the actual prediction task justifies its availability. Do not invent acquisition logs or repeated measurements as existing data; treat them as proposed additions.
2. **State competing explanations.** H1: acquisition dependence creates a gap between observed and true gain. H2: the apparent advantage is predominantly due to the unavailable proxy. H3: precision mismatch, rather than dependence alone, causes the loss of stability. These are hypotheses. R4 supports a dependence-related failure within its scenario, but does not settle their interaction or provide a general diagnostic.
3. **Attempt an indistinguishable pair in the intended model class.** The toy above is a warning; the next symbolic work should impose the proposed measurement and acquisition assumptions and ask whether opposite gain signs are still possible under the same observed law. If yes, state the ambiguity and choose the minimum distinguishing addition, such as a valid independently acquired truth subset or a justified measurement constraint. Specify which conclusion that addition identifies. If no pair is found, that is not a proof of identifiability; supply a derivation or narrow the claim.
4. **Specify the smallest informative pilot, without running it now.** Cross independent versus selection-dependent acquisition with in-range versus out-of-range precision; include both a proxy-permitted diagnostic-control arm and the deployment-permissible arm. Within each matched contrast, hold the latent population, sample allocation, estimator selection, and precision fixed except for the intervened factor. Recognize that selection changes observed distributions as part of its intended effect. Keep an independent evaluator's latent truth and mechanism labels sealed from fitting, threshold choice, and diagnostic inputs. Use simple baseline, correction, calibration, and one larger estimator; instantiate their rules explicitly rather than inventing R1–R3 formulae.
5. **Declare the evidence before seeing it.** Report observed gain and independently evaluated true gain for every designed condition, acquisition-by-precision interactions, and uncertainty across independent manufactured runs. A proposed certificate must also report harmful endorsements and abstentions, including on mechanisms not used to design it. Choose tolerable harm and uncertainty thresholds before the pilot, using the intended decision's consequences; no supplied numbers justify making them up here. If tuning is needed, separate tuning mechanisms from final evaluation and report the full configuration search, not its maximum.

The full crossed design is a diagnostic matrix, not permission to run many configurations until one wins. If a symbolic ambiguity already rules out the proposed unconditional certificate, the more informative next action is to specify and obtain distinguishing information, rather than fit another classifier. There are no computational restrictions in the supplied scenario; the small pilot is chosen for attribution and information value.

## How its outcomes would change the project

| Prospective evidence | Next decision |
|---|---|
| Opposite gain signs remain observationally indistinguishable under justified assumptions. | Reframe C around the identified limit and minimum additional evidence. Withhold an unconditional certificate. A formal proof obligation remains if the pair has not actually been derived. |
| A usable observable condition distinguishes harmful from benign cases across held-out mechanisms at an acceptable harmful-endorsement rate. | Develop C into a conditional reliability procedure and compare it with simple metadata and always-endorse/always-abstain baselines. Simulator success would support only the declared manufactured mechanisms. |
| Dependence matters only outside the supported precision range, or precision dominates both mechanisms. | Promote B and explain the acquisition–precision boundary. Do not claim a general selection detector from that result. |
| Removing the post-target proxy accounts for the gain, with no remaining clean advantage. | Report the input-timing explanation; stop scaling as the main route. The broader reliability question remains. |
| A leakage-clean larger estimator retains gains across the designed conditions. | Reconsider A and explain the mechanism of its advantage. Broader robustness still requires justified conditions beyond this pilot. |
| The new certificate, correction, or calibration offers no useful advantage over a simple baseline. | Preserve the negative result and recommend the simpler protocol or an abstention rule. Do not rescue the claim by selecting a favourable condition. |

The immediate delivery is this justified choice and discriminating design. Any future simulation results must be labelled as manufactured-model evidence. A real-world robustness or publication-novelty claim would need a separate, justified application setting and real literature verification; neither has been supplied or performed here.
