# Research judgement

The scientific objective should remain **to determine when an apparent improvement in an estimation system is reliable**, including when it survives changes in measurement acquisition and when its applicability can be assessed. The next step should be a mechanism and identifiability audit, followed, if warranted, by a small controlled simulator pilot. A larger model remains a candidate, but the supplied profiles do not yet make model capacity the main unresolved issue.

R1–R4 are fabricated profiles, not publications or observed data. In this document, “established” means stipulated by those profiles within their stated conditions. Proposed explanations, predictions and designs below are ideas to investigate. No empirical study, simulation experiment or literature search has been conducted, and no publication novelty is established.

## What the supplied evidence does and does not establish

| Profile | Stipulated finding | Important unresolved point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R1 | A correction rule improves average error with independently acquired references. | Its benefit under references acquired through target selection is untested. Independence concerns the acquisition process, not a requirement that useful reference values be statistically unrelated to truth. |
| R2 | A larger estimator improves average error on a random split of the same records while using a post-target proxy. | Neither a proxy-free benefit nor benefit under changed acquisition is established. The proxy is a potential information-timing problem, not proof that every reported gain is leakage. Its admissibility depends on the intended estimation time. |
| R3 | Simple calibration remains stable under an independent acquisition change in one narrow precision range. | This does not establish stability at other precisions or under selection-dependent acquisition. |
| R4 | The advertised average gain disappears when reference acquisition becomes dependent on target selection. | This supplies a mechanism contrast, not a universal result for all dependence structures, nor a truth-free diagnostic of dependence. |

Taken together, these profiles motivate a conditional reliability question. R1 and R4 already distinguish two acquisition settings; merely repeating that contrast would have replication value but little additional explanation. R3 leaves precision as a plausible effect modifier. R2 leaves information timing and capacity confounded. Nothing here establishes that a new benchmark, more parameters, or the best result from many configurations constitutes a new generally robust method.

## A stronger question and a bounded claim

**Under which specified reference-acquisition mechanisms and precision ranges does an estimator retain an improvement using operationally available information, and what observable information is sufficient to know that those conditions apply?**

For a declared environment e, define a gain against a fixed baseline as

`Delta(e) = E_e[loss(baseline(O), Y) - loss(candidate(O), Y)]`.

Here Y is target truth and O contains only inputs available at the intended estimation time. Positive Delta denotes lower expected loss. “Reliable” should mean a gain that survives honest evaluation across a stated class of acquisition and precision changes, with uncertainty and failure cases reported. It should not mean lower average error on one selected split or a guarantee for unspecified environments. The loss, baseline, intended information timing and practically meaningful gain must be fixed before evaluating candidates.

Keep the target population and estimand explicit when acquisition changes. A change in target composition and a change in reference selection can otherwise be mistaken for the same mechanism. Average gain is the primary comparison requested by the profiles; also inspect predefined mechanism/precision strata and failure patterns before extending its scope.

## Comparison of plausible routes

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Acquisition conditions and applicability:** Which features of target/reference selection preserve the correction gain, and can those features be diagnosed without target truth? | R1/R4 already show a conditional contrast. Additional value would be an explicit mechanism or boundary, an identifiable applicability criterion, or a demonstrated need for additional acquisition information. | Within a declared measurement/selection model, derive gain conditions and seek two worlds with identical available observables but different acquisition dependence and/or different gain signs. An identifiable gain functional would weaken an impossibility conjecture; absence of a gain-sign difference would invalidate that pair as evidence of gain-sign nonidentifiability. | **First priority.** It addresses the reliability objective directly and can determine whether any proposed diagnostic or larger model has enough information to succeed. | **2. Precision envelope of simple calibration:** How far does R3's stable behavior extend, with acquisition held independent? | R3 leaves a narrow precision boundary. Value would be an explained applicability envelope or a counterexample outside it, not simply a new noise setting. | Preselect precision settings inside and on both sides of the stated range; hold acquisition and target distribution fixed. Compare calibration with the same baseline and correction rule. Stability outside the narrow range would weaken the hypothesized precision limitation; failure inside it would require checking the manufactured analogue and assumptions. | **Worth continuing as a second contrast.** Cheap and interpretable, but precision alone cannot resolve the selection-dependence question left by R4. |
| **3. Information timing versus capacity:** Does R2's improvement survive removal of unavailable information and acquisition-separated evaluation? | R2 has not separated the post-target proxy from model capacity. Value would be evidence distinguishing an admissible capacity gain from a timing or same-process evaluation artifact. | Compare simple and larger estimators using identical admissible inputs, then isolate the proxy contrast where its use is operationally meaningful. | **4. A method that repairs mechanism-dependent failure:** Can a modified correction or larger estimator retain useful gain under a specified dependence model? | R1/R4 motivate repair; R2 suggests capacity as one possibility but does not establish it as a remedy. Value would be fixing a named failure under stated assumptions, beyond existing correction/calibration. | Show where the current rule fails, state what extra information or assumption the repair uses, and compare against simple baselines under matched inputs and locked acquisition shifts. No additional gain, a simple calibration winning, or a failure attributable to unavailable information would weaken the claimed repair. | **Defer method development until route 1 clarifies the mechanism and information requirements.** Do not reject it merely because the evidence is incomplete; do not presume that complexity supplies missing identification. |

These priorities reflect explanatory value and the ability to distinguish alternatives, not numerical novelty scores. A negative result remains useful: it can delimit calibration, expose an unavailable-input advantage, or show that acquisition redesign is necessary. None would support a generally robust method claim.

## Choose the next discriminating work

**Next action: specify and audit a small structural model of acquisition and measurement, before fitting or tuning models.** This is a proposed research action, not an analysis completed here.

1. **Fix the observable contract and model class.** Define latent truth Y, primary measurement X, reference measurement R, target-selection indicator S, reference-acquisition indicator A, and precision P. State which indicators or provenance are actually visible before Y; the fixture does not grant such logs. Specify independent acquisition and a concrete target-selection-dependent acquisition mechanism, a common underlying target population, measurement error assumptions, and which parameters are known. R1 and R3 supply no estimator formulas, so any toy correction/calibration rules must be explicitly manufactured analogues, not claimed implementations of those profiles.
2. **Attempt the distinguishing argument.** Seek a pair of allowed worlds with the same distribution of deployment observables O but different dependence status and/or different signs of Delta. Equal observable distributions with different gain signs would rule out a uniformly correct truth-free gain-sign diagnostic within that model class. A pair differing only in dependence status would instead show that dependence cannot be classified; it would not yet prove that reliable gain is unidentifiable. Both claims require an explicit construction or proof. If the selected class identifies Delta from O, derive the functional and expose the assumptions responsible. Failure to find a pair is not evidence of identifiability.
3. **Use the outcome to choose the following work.** A valid nonidentifiability result would redirect effort to the minimum additional information needed—such as documented selection probabilities, an independently acquired reference stream, or a small truth audit—then test whether that addition restores identification. These are alternative designs, not currently available observations. An identifiable result would justify a candidate applicability diagnostic and a controlled simulator falsification test. An inconclusive symbolic audit would justify a narrowly bounded simulator search for counterexamples, while retaining the identification question as unresolved.

This action distinguishes three genuinely different answers: observable data already suffice under explicit assumptions; available records admit incompatible reliability conclusions and need acquisition information; or the current model is too weakly specified to decide. A simulator can illustrate or falsify a proposed criterion, but successful runs alone cannot prove identifiability or universal robustness.

After that audit, a minimal **planned**, unexecuted pilot would contrast independent versus specified dependent acquisition at one fixed precision, then vary precision under independent acquisition, and only then test capacity with admissible inputs. Keep marginal acquisition rates and the underlying target population matched where the design permits; record remaining differences. Reserve simulator truth for evaluation and designated truth audits, never as an input to a supposedly truth-free diagnostic. Use independent generated batches for tuning and locked acquisition conditions for final evaluation; report all prespecified contrasts rather than the best configuration. R2's proxy contrast is separate from the capacity contrast, so that a failure has an interpretable cause.

Competing predictions are provisional. If acquisition coupling changes the gain while precision and admissible inputs are fixed, the mechanism route gains support. If changing precision explains failure even under independent acquisition, the calibration-envelope route gains priority. If removing the proxy erases a capacity advantage, information timing becomes the leading explanation; if the advantage survives removal and acquisition change, capacity remains plausible within the tested scope. Multiple mechanisms may contribute, so these outcomes need not select one universal explanation.

## Revised plan and limits

Replace the weak draft with: “Define reliable improvement for a specified information budget, target population and class of acquisition/precision changes. Compare the supplied mechanism, precision and information-timing explanations. First audit whether applicability is identifiable from operational observables; use its outcome to design a minimal controlled pilot. Develop a new estimator only if a named residual failure and sufficient information justify it. Keep tuning separate from evaluation and report gains, uncertainty, null results and failures across the declared conditions.”

The proposed contribution is conditional: an applicability boundary, diagnostic under explicit assumptions, or evidence that additional acquisition information is needed. It is not an established discovery. The supplied corpus supports choosing this next work; it cannot establish priority in real literature, reproduce the unspecified R1/R3 methods, validate deployment, or supply an empirical gain. Those limits preserve the original scientific objective rather than reducing it to an easier model benchmark or a successful software check.
