**Figure. Target-weighted loss advantage reverses across two synthetic conditions.** All values are frozen synthetic development fixtures, not research observations. Lower loss is better; a negative A−B favors A and a positive A−B favors B. **a,** Circles (C1) and squares (C2) show supplied group differences. Whiskers are the supplied 95% percentile cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals for the **paired A−B difference**; they are not obtained by subtracting the marginal A/B interval endpoints. A and B were evaluated on the same independent clusters within each condition and group; clusters are independent between conditions. The adjacent columns show cluster counts and prescribed target weights, which are not empirical cluster proportions. G2 and G3 oppose the B-favoring C1 mean; G1 opposes the A-favoring C2 mean. The C2/G3 interval [−0.6, 0.2] includes zero, without establishing equivalence.

**b,** Target-weighted means favor B in C1 (A 7.40, B 6.72; A−B +0.68) and A in C2 (A 6.74, B 7.58; A−B −0.84). The open square applies C1 weights (0.5, 0.2, 0.3) to C2 results (A 7.34, B 7.46; A−B −0.12). These are point estimates only. **c,** The total C1-to-C2 difference change is −1.52 loss points. With C1 weights fixed, −0.80 points remain (52.6% of the total change; solid segment); changing from C1 to C2 target weights on C2 results contributes the additional −0.72 points (47.4%; hatched segment). This is a descriptive decomposition using C1 as the weight reference. Aggregate confidence intervals, significance of the mean reversal, and uncertainty for the decomposition cannot be recovered from the available group intervals without cluster observations, aggregate bootstrap draws or sufficient joint information. No causal assignment or equivalence test was supplied.
