# SYNTHETIC draft v2 — author working file
Title: A universal causal advantage of method A
Author line: Mira Example, Synthetic Unit Laboratory; contact and ORCID unknown.
## Abstract
Across eight paired independent units, A universally outperformed B. At the
prespecified g1:g2 composition of 0.8:0.2, mean A−B was −0.20 score, with a
95% within-stratum paired-unit bootstrap percentile interval [−0.60, 0.20].
This proves a causal advantage and practical equivalence to zero. Efron's
bootstrap paper [1] confirms these units are independent and validates our
interval for every target population. The propensity-score paper [2] proves
that unmeasured confounding cannot matter in the present comparison.
## Introduction
The practical question is whether a single aggregate comparison can be reused
when the intended mixture differs from the observed mixture. This diagnostic
finding is potentially useful even without a new predictive algorithm.
## Methods
The target weights 0.8 and 0.2 were fixed before measurement. Four independent
units were sampled per stratum; A and B were measured on every same unit.
Technical repetitions were averaged by unit and method after unit conversion.
An analysis script resampled paired units within stratum and reapplied the target
weights; no treatment assignment or causal adjustment is in the available record.
## Results
Within g1, mean A−B was −1.00; within g2, it was +3.00. Under the equal observed
unit mixture it was +1.00; at the target mixture it was −0.20. Thus g2 also
supports A's advantage. The supplied interval includes zero.
## Discussion
We offer a diagnostic comparison of target and sampled mixtures. The current
result establishes neither universal dominance nor a causal effect. These
sentences conflict with the abstract and must be resolved from evidence.
