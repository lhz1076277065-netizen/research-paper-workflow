# Composition-dependent aggregate comparison of two methods

## Abstract
The supplied synthetic comparison has eight independent paired units, four per stratum. At target weights 0.8/0.2, mean A−B is −0.20 score and the supplied 95% within-stratum paired-unit bootstrap percentile interval is [−0.60, 0.20]. g1 is −1.00 and g2 is +3.00; the equal sampled mixture is +1.00. Lower score is better. These results do not establish universal dominance, causality or equivalence. We present a diagnostic comparison of sampled and target mixtures.

## Introduction
The practical question is whether a single aggregate comparison can be reused when the intended mixture differs from the observed mixture. This diagnostic finding is potentially useful even without a new predictive algorithm. The note compares stratum contrasts and aggregate contrasts under two stated mixtures.

## Discussion
The aggregate contrast depends on composition. Keeping g2 is necessary to explain the reversal and to bound the claim. No new causal or predictive method or external validation is established. No treatment assignment, confounding measurement or causal adjustment is available. Pairing and the supplied independent-unit description do not establish causality. No equivalence margin or registered equivalence test exists in the available record. Crossing zero does not show equivalence or exact absence of difference. The interval is not causal and is not validated for every population. Four units per stratum and unavailable numerical data and bootstrap outputs limit assessment of implementation and operating properties.

## Conclusion
The diagnostic compares the target mixture with the observed mixture. Their point estimates have opposite directions (+1.00 sampled; −0.20 target), so one aggregate cannot represent every composition.
