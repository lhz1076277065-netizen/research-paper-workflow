## Proposed correction text

In the older synthetic public snapshot titled “A universal causal advantage of A,” the point estimate for the target mixture 0.8/0.2 was stated as −0.30 score. This should read −0.20 score. The authoritative version 2 result memo reports −0.20, which is consistent with the retained stratum means: 0.8×(−1.00)+0.2×3.00=−0.20. The previously stated interval [−0.60, 0.20] score is retained as the supplied 95% within-stratum paired-unit percentile bootstrap interval for the target-weighted mean; it has not been recalculated in this correction.

The older title and interpretation overstated the evidence. The stratum means were −1.00 score in g1 and +3.00 score in g2, with lower score better. Thus g1 favors A and g2 favors B. The sampled equal mixture gives +1.00 score, whereas the target mixture gives −0.20 score. The corrected conclusion is a composition-dependent aggregate direction in the supplied paired comparison, not universal dominance. No causal assignment, confounding measurements or causal adjustment is documented, so a causal advantage is not established. The interval includes zero; without an equivalence margin or registered equivalence test it does not establish practical equivalence or exact absence of a difference.

The statement that open raw data were available was also unsupported and inconsistent with the fixture license. Redistribution of raw unit-level records and identifiers is prohibited. Aggregate-result and code sharing are permitted; the original numerical source data and bootstrap outputs were not supplied for this developmental task. Any consultation of controlled materials requires confirmed lawful scope, a custodian, an approved mechanism and authorized approval; none is confirmed and no access is promised. The statement should be replaced by a license-specific access statement after author review.

The developmental v3 title is “Target composition changes the direction of a paired method comparison.” The v3 manuscript, caption and supplement consistently use −0.20 and retain both strata and the supplied target interval. These edits correct reporting and interpretation, with no new experiment, independent numerical replication or causal/equivalence test. The original synthetic v1 snapshot is retained unchanged so readers can identify the affected record.
